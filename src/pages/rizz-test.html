    How the challenge works

    • One fictional chat. Sam is a made-up character on a dating app. Every reply Sam sends is pre-written.
    • Three messages. Each round you choose one of four replies, from low-effort to genuinely good.
    • Instant feedback. You get a score out of 100, your strongest habit, one habit to watch, and a practical tip linked to the lesson that covers it.
    • Challenge a friend. Share a link with your score (never your replies) and see if they can beat you.

    How scoring works

    The score comes from a simple rubric based on the principles in our lessons. Replies earn more points when they:

    • ask a playful, easy-to-answer question,
    • pick up on something the other person said or wrote (a callback),
    • suggest a clear plan with a day, time or place,
    • respect it when someone says they’re busy.

    Replies lose points for one-word answers, firing off lists of questions, big compliments before any real conversation, talking only about yourself, and vague plans. Pushing after someone says they’re busy scores zero.

    This is an opinionated practice game, not a scientific test. It can’t predict how any real person will respond, and real conversations are more forgiving than a score suggests. For the thinking behind it, see how to text someone you like.

    Quick answers

    What is a rizz test?

    “Rizz” is slang for charm or flirting skill. This rizz test is a short practice game: one fictional chat, three replies and feedback on what worked and what didn’t. More on what rizz means.

    Is the score scientific?

    No. It’s a transparent rubric written by Flirt Lessons to reward playful, curious and respectful replies. Treat it as practice and a bit of fun, not a measurement of you.

    Do you store my answers?

    No. The challenge runs in your browser and your replies are never sent or saved. We only count anonymous totals, such as how many people finished and in which 10-point score range, and share links contain only your score.
