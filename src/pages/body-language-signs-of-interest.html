The signals at a glance

Green: they’re enjoying it

Leaning in, steady eye contact, asking you questions, touching your arm back, staying when they could easily leave.

Amber: give more space

Polite but short replies, glancing around, laughing less. Ease off and reconnect through conversation.

Red: stop

Pulling away, crossed arms, body turned away, stiffening at touch, mentioning a partner or needing to leave. No touch. Be gracious.

Look for clusters, not single signs

One sign on its own means very little. Crossed arms might just mean the room is cold; a long look might just mean they’re trying to remember where they know you from. What matters is a cluster of signals that points the same way, repeated over time. If several green signs show up together and keep showing up, interest is likely. If you’re genuinely unsure, the simplest move is to ask them out clearly and let them tell you.

Your own body language

  • Open posture. Face them, uncross your arms, keep your shoulders relaxed.
  • Comfortable eye contact. Hold it while they talk, glance away naturally. Staring feels intense; avoiding their eyes reads as nervous.
  • Phone away. Nothing signals interest like full attention.
  • Smile with your eyes. Real enjoyment shows; you don’t need to force it.

Touch: small steps, always mutual

Friendly touch, like a high-five after a good joke, can show you’re comfortable with someone. But it only works when it’s welcome. Start with the kind of touch that would be normal between friends, watch how they respond, and let them match you before going any further. If they don’t, that’s an answer, not a puzzle to solve.

A few ground rules: comfort can change at any moment, so keep checking in. Someone who has had a lot to drink can’t give clear consent, so keep things friendly. And a “no”, spoken or unspoken, is always the end of that conversation.

When to stop

If you see red signals, step back immediately and keep things warm and light. Respecting a signal gracefully is attractive in itself: it shows you’re secure and safe to be around. It helps to decide on an exit line in advance, such as “It was really nice meeting you, have a great night,” so you’re never tempted to push.

Quick answers

How can I tell flirting from friendliness?

Friendliness tends to be the same with everyone; flirting is singled out and escalates. Look for more personal questions, extra one-on-one attention and signs that build over time. When in doubt, ask them on a clear one-on-one date.

What if I misread the signals?

It happens to everyone. Apologize briefly and sincerely, give them space, and don’t make it awkward by over-explaining. Handling it well is far more important than never getting it wrong.
