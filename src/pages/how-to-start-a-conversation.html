What works in the first minute

  • Comment on your shared surroundings. The music, the menu, the absurd queue. It’s real, it’s easy to answer and it puts you on the same team.
  • Tease the situation, not the person. “You ordered that with a lot of confidence” invites a playful comeback. Keep it warm, and if a joke misses, own it with a smile and move on.
  • Use open, relaxed body language. Easy eye contact, loose shoulders, a voice with some energy. Use their name once you know it.
  • Ask one more question than usual. Follow-ups like “Wait, how did you end up doing that?” show you’re actually listening.
  • Drop the audition. You’re not trying to win approval. You’re finding out if you click, and that shift alone calms nerves.

Example openers for everyday places

Café

“Okay, settle this for me: is the oat milk worth the extra?”

Bookshop

“I need a recommendation. If you could only reread one book, what would it be?”

Party

“How do you know the host? I’m trying to work out which part of their life everyone’s from.”

Queue

“This line is moving like it has somewhere better to be.”

Dog park

“Your dog is clearly the main character here. What’s their name?”

Class or workshop

“Be honest, are you actually following this, or are you just better at hiding it than me?”

Notice what they have in common: each one is about something you can both see, invites an easy answer and has a little warmth or humor in it.

Keeping it going

  • Follow the thread. “How”, “why” and “what was that like” questions turn small talk into real conversation.
  • Share as well as ask. After a couple of questions, offer something about yourself so it doesn’t feel like an interview.
  • Watch their energy. Longer answers and questions back mean they’re enjoying it. Short replies and glances away mean it’s time to wrap up kindly.

How to end it well

Leave while the conversation is still good. If you’d like to see them again, say so simply: “I’ve really enjoyed this. Can I get your number and take you for coffee sometime?” Whatever the answer, respond warmly. A graceful exit is part of a good first impression.

Key ideaPresence beats lines. Curious, relaxed and a little playful is the whole formula.

Quick answers

What if I get nervous and my mind goes blank?

That happens to everyone. Take a slow breath, look around and comment on something you can see. You can also simply ask a question about them; most people enjoy talking about their own lives.

How do I know if they want to keep talking?

They turn their body toward you, give longer answers, ask questions back and don’t look for an exit. If replies get shorter and their attention drifts, thank them and let the conversation end on a good note.
